# Let an album do the sorting

This report covers two people who want to share a selected subset of their photos at no cost. One uses an iPhone, a Mac and iCloud (the iPhone user). The other uses an Android phone, a Windows laptop and OneDrive (the Android user) and is willing to do most of the work. Both are near their free Google Photos limits.

The best free answer is to stop trying to sync or classify and give the iPhone user exactly one gesture, adding photos to a single album in Apple Photos, while the Android user handles everything downstream. Since iOS 27 (September 2026) an iCloud shared album owned by the iPhone user accepts full-resolution uploads from an Android phone or a Windows browser with only an emailed code, and a "temporary" variant uses no iCloud storage for 30 days. The first thing to try therefore needs no code: the iPhone user adds trip photos to a shared album from the share sheet, the Android user downloads them into a OneDrive shared folder they own, and that folder stays the permanent joint album on the Android user's quota. If the manual download becomes tedious, or the iPhone is not on iOS 27, the next step is a small pipeline: osxphotos on the Mac exporting an ordinary album to the OneDrive folder on a schedule, with icloudpd on the Windows laptop as the fallback. Google Photos is blocked by both quotas, iCloud Shared Photo Library is Apple-only, OneDrive's iPhone camera upload is all-or-nothing, and self-hosting adds a server without removing the iPhone user's step. An automatic filter is feasible, fast, local and effectively free, but the transformer is the minor part: Apple Photos metadata already flags screenshots, the app each image was saved from, trips and home, whereas "personal versus joint" is not in the pixels, so an estimated 5 to 15% of items would still need a human glance. None of these flows was tested hands-on; the iOS and macOS versions, the iCloud headroom and the OneDrive plan decide which branch applies.

## iOS 27 turned the Photos app into a cross-platform, full-resolution drop box

The landscape changed in September 2026. Apple rebuilt Shared Albums in iOS 27, iPadOS 27 and macOS 27 so that friends and family can "join and contribute their own photos on the web, even without an Apple Account or Apple device" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108314)). On the Android side the flow is a link: the album owner picks "approved people only" or "anyone with the link", the recipient opens it in a browser, verifies a one-time email code, and can then view, download single photos or all photos, comment and add their own, provided the owner sets them to "Can Add Photos" ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2026/09/17/icloud-shared-album-android/)). There is no Android app; the browser is the client, and both people see each other's full email address ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2026/09/17/icloud-shared-album-android/)). For the iPhone user nothing changes from earlier years: select, Share, add to shared album.

The catch is storage. Albums created in version 27 or later keep items **at full resolution in original formats and count toward the owner's iCloud storage, including what participants add**, while albums created in version 26 or earlier stay storage-free but reduce photos to **2,048 px** on the long edge and video to 720p ([Apple Support: limits](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/148868)). The escape hatch is the new **temporary shared album: it expires after 30 days, uses no iCloud storage, does not require iCloud Photos, and holds up to 5,000 items**; anything not saved to a personal library by then is deleted, and the feature is not available in every region ([Apple Support: temporary albums](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/127875)). Pre-release reporting says an owner's own library photos are not counted twice in a permanent album, so only the other participant's uploads would add to the owner's usage, but that is beta-era and unconfirmed ([iDrop News](https://www.idropnews.com/ios-27/ios-27-icloud-shared-albums-overhaul/266144/)). Shared Albums are also excluded from end-to-end encryption even with Advanced Data Protection on ([Ente](https://ente.com/articles/ios-27-shared-albums/)).

That makes the durable home of the joint album a separate question from the iPhone user's input channel, and OneDrive is the right home. Microsoft states that shared folders "only count against the folder owner's storage space" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/onedrive/add-shortcuts-to-shared-folders-in-onedrive)), so an album kept in the Android user's OneDrive costs the iPhone user nothing. What OneDrive cannot do is collect from an iPhone selectively: camera backup uploads the whole camera roll, one way, to one account, and needs the app kept alive in the background ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/automatically-save-photos-and-videos-with-onedrive-on-ios-74d406bb-71d0-47c0-8ab8-98679fa1b72e)). The only selective OneDrive route from the iPhone is a manual upload per batch, which needs a Microsoft account and the app. Two cautions apply to the folder itself: edit rights let either person delete everything, and deleting a synced shortcut folder on a PC deletes it for everyone ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/onedrive/add-shortcuts-to-shared-folders-in-onedrive)), so the shared folder should hold copies, not the only originals. Reports conflict on whether a OneDrive "album" (as opposed to a folder) can be shared with add rights ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/a/12018710), [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/office/share-photos-5834308c-2115-40c3-a0bd-6aeba5cb1195)); a folder with "Can edit" is the safe construct.

The remaining hosted options lose on a constraint already stated. Google Photos charges every new upload, Storage saver included, to the uploader's 15 GB, and an account that runs out cannot save new items ([Google Photos Help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/10100180?hl=en)); partner sharing filters only by face group or start date, not by album ([Google Photos Help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/7378858?hl=en)). iCloud Shared Photo Library requires every participant to be signed in with an Apple Account with iCloud Photos on ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/mm14b8800edd/icloud)). The one third-party service worth keeping in reserve is Ente.

## No ready-made tool carries an Apple album to OneDrive, but three free extractors do

Searches found no maintained open-source project that syncs a chosen Apple Photos or iCloud album to OneDrive end to end; the nearest is an early-stage Swift app that copies albums to Google Photos ([mortht/photo-sync](https://github.com/mortht/photo-sync)). Automation therefore means composing an extractor with OneDrive. The design choice that matters most is the selection gesture, and the notes converge on an **ordinary (non-shared) album**: it syncs through iCloud Photos to both the Mac and the iCloud web API, so every extractor can read it, whereas shared albums are readable only by Apple's own clients, iCloud.com and, on the Mac, osxphotos since v0.77.2 ([osxphotos releases](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/releases)).

| Extractor | Runs on | iPhone user's ongoing effort | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| osxphotos v0.78.1 ([releases](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/releases)) | The Mac, scheduled | Add to album (or nothing with rule-based queries) | Reads Photos' SQLite database, so macOS schema changes break it until a new release; Mac must be awake |
| icloudpd v1.34.0 ([releases](https://github.com/icloud-photos-downloader/icloud_photos_downloader/releases)) | The Windows laptop, Task Scheduler | Add to album; approve 2FA about every two months | The Apple Account password and a whole-library session sit on another person's PC; unofficial Apple web API |
| rclone `iclouddrive` Photos mode ([docs](https://rclone.org/iclouddrive/)) | The Windows laptop | Add to album; 2FA code every 30 days | "Experimental" tier; not documented whether it returns unmodified originals |

osxphotos is the most capable. It exports originals by album, favourite, person, date, place, ML label and screenshot flag, incrementally with `--update`, and can pull iCloud-only originals with `--download-missing` ([osxphotos CLI](https://rhettbull.github.io/osxphotos/cli.html)). Its fragility is real: 2026 releases repeatedly patched new macOS schemas, most recently the macOS 27.3 beta on 8 November ([osxphotos releases](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/releases)), and launchd-run jobs can be denied Photos access without a prompt ([osxphotos discussion](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/discussions/1291)). Its decisive advantage is that the Apple credentials never leave the Mac. icloudpd is simpler to deploy: a standalone executable with `--album`, originals by default, and since v1.33.0 it works with Advanced Data Protection via `--adp-web-access`, but it needs "Access iCloud Data on the Web" enabled on the iPhone and Apple expires the session after about two months ([icloudpd authentication](https://icloud-photos-downloader.github.io/icloud_photos_downloader/authentication.html)). The project carries a "looking for maintainer" banner despite frequent releases ([icloudpd README](https://github.com/icloud-photos-downloader/icloud_photos_downloader)), and it does not read shared albums: that request has been open since December 2024 ([icloudpd issues](https://github.com/icloud-photos-downloader/icloud_photos_downloader/issues?q=is%3Aissue+shared+album)).

The Apple-side paths that look obvious do not hold up. iCloud for Windows syncs shared albums to a local folder automatically, but Apple ties it to legacy albums only, telling participants on non-current software to "view the album on iCloud.com" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/124160)); sign-in since version 14.2 requires a trusted Apple device nearby ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/102595)), and users report the shared-albums folder being wiped on reboot ([Apple Community](https://discussions-jp-prz.apple.com/thread/255040319)). An iPhone Shortcuts automation cannot save to OneDrive unattended according to community answers ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5691377/how-do-i-add-onedrive-as-a-location-to-save-photos)). PhotoSync does album-selective automatic transfer to OneDrive, but only in a paid tier, and its free tier transfers in low quality ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id415850124)). USB import requires originals on the device and is manual ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267)), and Apple's own transfer tool sends the whole library, once, to Google Photos only ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/118257)).

## Self-hosting and peer-to-peer add work on one side without removing it on the other

Immich is the strongest self-hosted candidate and it is not a toy: stable since October 2025, at v3 since July 2026, with native iOS and Android apps, album-selective phone backup and two-way shared albums ([Immich stable release](https://immich.app/blog/stable-release), [Immich v3.0.0](https://immich.app/blog/v3.0.0-release), [Immich mobile backup](https://docs.immich.app/features/mobile-backup), [Immich sharing](https://docs.immich.app/features/sharing)). The cost sits in the server: **6 GB RAM minimum, 4 GB with machine learning disabled, and Windows hosting "strongly discouraged"** ([Immich requirements](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements)). It only makes sense where an always-on Linux server is already available. Even then the iPhone user must install an app, enter a server URL, and accept that "the app cannot control when the background upload task will run" on iOS ([Immich mobile backup](https://docs.immich.app/features/mobile-backup)). The album would also be viewed in a new app rather than Photos. Self-hosted Ente is lighter at 1 GB RAM ([Ente self-hosting](https://ente.com/help/self-hosting/)), but Ente's hosted free tier makes self-hosting it pointless for a selected subset.

Peer-to-peer tools fail on iOS background limits. Syncthing reaches the iPhone only through Möbius Sync, which caps free use at 20 MB, offers photo folders as send-only and syncs roughly one to two hours a day ([Möbius Sync FAQ](https://mobiussync.com/faq)). Taildrop sends only between devices one user owns ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/kb/1106/taildrop)). LocalSend is a manual, foreground transfer ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1661733229)). These are fine for a one-off batch in the same room and wrong as a system.

## Metadata settles most of the filter; a transformer only helps in the middle

A filter can be written, and it should run on the Mac, where Apple has already done most of the classification. osxphotos exposes per-photo `screenshot` and `screen_recording` flags, `imported_by` (the name and bundle id of the app that saved the item), `syndicated` for "Shared with You" items, named `persons`, Apple's ML `labels`, aesthetic scores, and search facets including `trip`, `home` and document types ([osxphotos reference](https://rhettbull.github.io/osxphotos/reference.html)). The `imported_by` field is the user-visible "Saved from" information iOS has recorded since iOS 15 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2021/06/14/ios-15-photos-app-shows-where-images-saved-from)). Two of the three exclusion targets are therefore lookups, not predictions: screenshots carry a flag, and media saved from WhatsApp or Instagram carries the saving app's identity. On the Android and OneDrive side no such database exists, but WhatsApp's `IMG-YYYYMMDD-WAnnnn.jpg` naming is a documented pattern ([WhatsApp filename regex](https://metacpan.org/pod/Regexp::Pattern::Filename::Image::WhatsApp)).

The third target, personal photos that are not from holidays, splits in two. "Holiday" is a where-and-when question that Apple's `trip` facet, away-from-home GPS, or DBSCAN clustering of coordinates answers better than pixels ([Geoff Boeing](https://geoffboeing.com/?p=97)); note that Apple's Trips collection can lag by a week or more ([Apple Community](https://discussions-cn-prz.apple.com/thread/256192997)). "Joint life at home" is intent, and no image model sees intent. Who is in frame is the best proxy, and the named People in the Photos library supply it for free.

A transformer image model earns its place only on the residue. Accuracy on this exact task is unmeasured anywhere: no public benchmark covers share-or-not triage of a personal library. The nearest evidence is a supervised classifier on CLIP features that separated memes and screenshots at **93.3%** validation accuracy ([Memes in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04313)), and a practitioner who sorted about **70,000 personal images with zero-shot CLIP ViT-L/14 on an M1 MacBook Air in about three hours**, with roughly 1% falling below the confidence threshold into a review folder and no accuracy figure reported ([Sorting 70,000 photos with CLIP](https://pabasara-mahindapala.github.io/writing/sorting-70000-photos-with-clip/)). That is about 6.5 images per second, so a year of photos embeds in minutes to tens of minutes with a smaller current model such as SigLIP 2 B/16 (78.2% ImageNet zero-shot) or MobileCLIP2-S2 (77.2%) ([SigLIP 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14786v1), [MobileCLIP2](https://huggingface.co/timm/MobileCLIP2-S0-OpenCLIP/blob/main/README.md)). Training logistic regression on those embeddings from a few hundred of the users' own accept/reject decisions should beat zero-shot prompts, since zero-shot CLIP only matches a 16-shot linear probe on ImageNet ([CLIP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00020)); the size of that gain here is an inference. Local vision-language models are two orders of magnitude slower, 0.8 to 5.2 seconds per image on an RTX 4060 in PhotoPrism's August 2026 benchmark ([PhotoPrism](https://docs.photoprism.app/developer-guide/vision/model-comparison/)), and add little beyond a one-line reason for the reviewer. No throughput figure was found for a CPU-only Windows laptop.

Cost is not the constraint and privacy is. Local models cost nothing and nothing leaves the Mac. Cloud vision is cheap: at the cheapest listed tiers of US$0.10 to US$0.30 per million input tokens ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)) and roughly 230 to 280 tokens per downscaled image, the researchers' arithmetic gives about **US$0.30 to US$3 per 10,000 photos**. But paid APIs keep inputs for up to about 30 days ([Anthropic](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-is-my-data-used-for-model-training), [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), and Gemini's free tier uses submitted content to improve products, allows human review, and tells users not to submit personal information ([Gemini API terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). Sending someone's private photos to a cloud model buys almost nothing the local stack lacks.

The design that follows is a three-way decision, not a classifier verdict. Errors are asymmetric: a wrongly shared photo exposes something private, a wrongly omitted one is added later. Even Google warns that its face-filtered partner sharing occasionally shares photos without the selected people ([Google Photos Help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/7378858?hl=en)). So deterministic rules auto-exclude, high-confidence trip rules auto-include, and everything else lands in a "Proposed for sharing" album in Photos, defaulting to not shared, where a reviewer prunes it. The researchers' reasoned estimate, not a measurement, is that rules settle 70 to 90% of items, People plus an embedding classifier most of the rest, and **5 to 15% need a glance: 500 to 1,500 of 10,000 photos a year, or 15 to 45 minutes annually** when grouped by event. This creates a tension with the goal of minimal effort: someone must review, and the least intrusive reviewer is the owner of the photos. An explicit album costs the owner less effort than reviewing a classifier's leftovers, which is why the album ranks first and the classifier last.

## Ranked recommendation: an album first, a Mac-side export second, a classifier third

**Try first, with no code.** The iPhone user creates one iCloud shared album per trip (or one running album) on iOS 27 and adds photos from the share sheet. If the iCloud plan has headroom, make it permanent and let it double as the album browsed in Photos; on the free tier or a tight plan, tick "temporary". The Android user opens the link on a laptop, downloads everything into a OneDrive shared folder they own, and adds their own photos there from the already-synced Pictures folder, uploading them into the iCloud album only if they are wanted in the iPhone user's Photos library. OneDrive remains the permanent album on the Android user's quota. This replaces sessions at the Mac with one download per trip and costs the iPhone user a few taps. If the iPhone is not on iOS 27, substitute an Ente album owned by the Android user with guest uploads enabled, or upload into the OneDrive folder from the OneDrive iOS app.

**Build second, if the download step becomes a nuisance.** The iPhone user switches to an ordinary album named for the purpose. On the Mac, a pinned osxphotos install runs under a LaunchAgent or a Shortcuts time-of-day automation, exports that album with `--update` to a plain local folder, and rclone (or the OneDrive Mac client) pushes it to the shared folder. Keep icloudpd on the Windows laptop pointed at the same album as the fallback for weeks when a macOS update breaks osxphotos, accepting that it stores the Apple Account password on that machine and needs a 2FA approval about every two months. Never use either tool's delete or move flags.

**Build third, only if maintaining an album is too much, or to mine the backlog.** Add the rule stage on the Mac: exclude screenshots, screen recordings, items saved by messenger, social and browser apps, hidden items and Apple-flagged documents; include camera originals inside trips; write candidates to a "Proposed" album. Run it once as a dry run to replace the estimated percentages with real ones. Add People, then a local SigLIP 2 or MobileCLIP2 embedding with a logistic-regression probe trained on logged decisions. Skip cloud APIs and skip Immich.

## What remains unverified, and the setup facts that flip the answer

No pipeline in this report was executed; every effort estimate comes from documentation. The specific unknowns that matter are these. Real-world reliability of the iOS 27 web flow for Android and Windows users has no forum evidence yet, and Apple does not document session length, upload batch limits, how multi-item download is delivered, or whether EXIF and location survive. Whether iCloud for Windows receives new-style albums, and whether an Apple Account with no Apple device can sign in to it, is untested. The claim that an owner's own library items are not double-counted in a permanent album is beta-era press. No Microsoft page states outright that a contributor's upload is charged to the folder owner; it rests on the shortcut-page sentence. Whether rclone's Photos mode returns unmodified originals is undocumented. osxphotos behaviour on a given macOS version, with "Optimise Mac Storage" on and under launchd permissions, is unknown, as is the reliability of `imported_by` and the `trip` facet on macOS 26 and 27. Every classifier accuracy and review-share figure is an estimate. The Google and OpenAI price cells were read through a summarising tool and should be re-checked before anyone relies on them, and Ente's own help pages conflict on whether free accounts can share albums, with the current page saying yes.

| Fact about the setup | Why it changes the answer |
| --- | --- |
| iOS and macOS version on the Apple side | New-style and temporary albums need version 27 to create ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/127875)); below that, go straight to the osxphotos or Ente branch. osxphotos support for macOS 27 is beta-level ([osxphotos](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos/releases)). |
| iCloud plan, free space, and whether iCloud Photos and "Optimise Mac Storage" are on | Decides permanent versus temporary shared album; optimised storage forces the slower, less reliable `--download-missing` path ([osxphotos README](https://github.com/RhetTbull/osxphotos)). |
| Whether Advanced Data Protection is on | icloudpd and rclone then need "Access iCloud Data on the Web" enabled, a security trade-off the account owner must accept ([icloudpd](https://icloud-photos-downloader.github.io/icloud_photos_downloader/authentication.html)). |
| OneDrive plan and free space | Free accounts get 5 GB; Microsoft 365 Family moves to a shared 2 TB pool for existing subscribers at renewal from 2 May 2027 ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/microsoft-storage-faqs-8fca6272-3427-4cce-a437-2c5ed84550a9)). With only 5 GB, Ente's 10 GB or a permanent iCloud album becomes the home. |
| Android phone model | Quick Share to AirDrop works only on a listed set of recent Pixel, Galaxy and other flagships ([Android](https://www.android.com/intl/en_uk/quick-share/with-iphone/)). |
| How often the Mac is awake, and whether it is Apple Silicon | A rarely opened Mac favours icloudpd on Windows; the speed figures above assume Apple Silicon. |
| Whether both people are named in Photos People and a home address is set | Without these, the "joint life at home" signal disappears and the review pile grows. |
| Yearly volume, especially video | Ente's 10 GB and a tight iCloud plan fill quickly with video. |
| Whether an always-on Linux server with about 6 GB RAM spare is available | The only condition under which Immich is worth a look ([Immich](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements)). |

## Conclusion

The question looks like a sync problem with a machine-learning garnish, and the research inverts it. Selection is the hard part, the person who owns the photos is the only one who knows which belong to the shared set, and Apple's September 2026 rebuild of Shared Albums made the cheapest selection gesture, adding to an album, finally reachable from Android and Windows at full quality. Automation is worth building only around that gesture, never instead of it.

The classifier idea survives in a narrower and more useful form: a rule-based proposer that mines years of backlog and shrinks a review pile, with a transformer as a late refinement. An hour's dry run of the metadata rules on a real library would replace every estimate here with a measured number, and should precede any model work.
